## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

An automated NinjaTrader 8 strategy that trades Micro Nasdaq futures (MNQ) during the US cash session using the published "noise-area" intraday momentum method (Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, 2024, *Beat the Market*, SSRN 4824172). Each day it draws a band around the open that represents normal noise; if price breaks out above the band it goes long, below it goes short, trails the position with the band and VWAP, reverses on a crossover to the opposite band, and is always flat at 16:00 ET. Fixed rules, fixed parameters, 1 contract. It does not learn or tune itself; every attempt to make it do so was tested and lost (see §9).

**Status (2026-10-16):** validated, audited four times, parity-proven against NinjaTrader trade for trade, approved in writing by Bulenox, running on the Sim101 paper account since 2026-10-15, monitored by scheduled checks twice a day. The final audit's fixes (partial sessions, account/strategy desync guard, daily-stop persistence, history guard) were compiled on 2026-10-18 (F5; the 2026-10-16 trade ran on the earlier build) and the post-compile Analyzer parity run on 2026-10-19 matched 1,666 of 1,666 trades (net $24,170.50 vs $24,232, −0.25%).

## 2. The rules (exact)

Times are US Eastern. Bars are 5-minute, stamped at their close (the bar stamped 09:35 covers 09:30–09:35). Regular session = 09:30–16:00.

**Volatility (sigma) per time of day**
For each time-of-day slot HH:MM, sigma = the average over the previous 14 valid sessions of `|Close(HH:MM) / Open(09:30) − 1|`.
A session is valid if its first RTH bar is stamped 09:35 and it has at least 30 RTH bars. If a slot is missing in some of the 14 sessions (early-close days), average the sessions that have it; at least 7 of 14 must have it, otherwise no decision at that slot. *(This was fixed 2026-10-15 after an independent re-implementation; before, one missing slot disabled all afternoon checks for 14 sessions.)*

**Noise area**
`Upper = max(Open 09:30, previous session's 16:00 close) × (1 + sigma)`
`Lower = min(Open 09:30, previous session's 16:00 close) × (1 − sigma)`
Volatility multiplier = 1.0 (base case).

**VWAP** = cumulative Σ((High+Low+Close)/3 × Volume) / Σ Volume from the 09:35 bar, cash session only.

**Decisions** are made at the close of the bars stamped 10:00, 10:30, 11:00 … 15:30 (every 30 minutes). Nothing is decided on other bars.
- Flat: close > Upper → go long; close < Lower → go short.
- Long: close < Lower → reverse to short; otherwise close < max(Upper, VWAP) → exit.
- Short: close > Upper → reverse to long; otherwise close > min(Lower, VWAP) → exit.
- A decision at a bar close is executed with a market order, which fills at the next bar's open.

**Flat at the close**: any position is closed on the 16:00 bar. Nothing is held overnight. Nothing trades outside 09:30–16:00 (the overnight session was tested and rejected, §9).

**Partial sessions**: CME equity index futures close at **13:00 ET** on US cash-market holidays when Globex still trades (MLK Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day observed, Labor Day, Thanksgiving) and at **13:15 ET** on the day before Independence Day (when a weekday), the day after Thanksgiving and Dec 24 (weekday; a full closure when Christmas is a Saturday). About nine a year; the backtest traded them. On such a day the strategy is flat on the bar that closes 5 minutes before the session end (12:55 or 13:10) and makes no decision at or after it. Source 1: NinjaTrader's Trading Hours template (read through SessionIterator, so an unlisted date cannot slip through if the template is current). Source 2: the indicator's `EarlyCloseDates` backup list (`yyyy-MM-dd` for 13:00, `yyyy-MM-dd=1315` for 13:15; default list covers 2026-11-26 through 2027-11-26). Update the list each December; the daily review derives the calendar independently and flags any decision made too late. NinjaTrader's "Exit on session close" stays on as a third net. Next: Thursday 2026-11-26 (13:00), Friday 2026-11-27 (13:15), Thursday 2026-12-24 (13:15).

**Costs assumed in all tests**: 1 tick slippage per side, $0.50 commission per side, 1 contract.

## 6. Daily operation

**What happens automatically**
- 09:12 ET weekdays (arrives about 09:17) — *pre-flight* (Claude scheduled task, read-only): heartbeat fresh, connected, running; contract-roll reminder in expiry week; early-close and exchange-holiday notes. Message only if something is wrong, plus one line when fine.
- 10:00–15:30 — the strategy decides every 30 minutes; the chart shows arrows and plays a sound on a position change.
- 16:00 — flat.
- 16:24 ET weekdays (arrives about 16:38) — *daily review* (read-only): the `daily_review.py` report. Verdict: All good / Warnings / NEEDS ATTENTION. Fridays: robustness suite on the accumulated live trades once there are 50.
- 14:40 ET weekdays (arrives about 14:43; moved from 14:20 on 2026-10-20 so it sits between the 14:30 and 15:00 decisions) — *restart test prompt*: silent until a day the strategy holds a position, then walks you through one disable/enable and verifies the position was re-adopted (PASSED requires proof of a restart — a new live_since and trade log — and the account matching the strategy's position). Delete this task once it has passed.
- January 5 — *annual re-validation*: last year's live trades vs the historical range (push).

**What you do**
- Leave it alone. Do not place manual trades on the strategy's account: the strategy cannot see them, and if it ever finds the account's position different from its own it stops sending orders, alerts, and reports `desync` until you reconcile by hand. Use another account for hand trading.
- Alarms in the review (2026-10-19, `research\MONITOR_RESULTS.txt`): **"edge watch"** (warning) = live results are running below the 16-year record; keep running, it is on in about a third of years by chance. **"edge-decay alarm"** (NEEDS ATTENTION) = stop and review; it false-alarms about once a decade, catches a broken strategy in about three months and a vanished edge in about two years, and on the record fired in Dec 2010 and Sep 2016, both before recoveries. **"fill cost … demonstrably above the 1.5 gate"** (NEEDS ATTENTION) = check order routing and latency; **"fill cost … above the gate (not yet conclusive)"** (warning) = keep watching; the 10:00 sides are shown separately.
- Read the daily-review message (about 16:38). "All good" needs nothing. Closing NinjaTrader after the close is fine: the review then says "strategy stopped after the close" and the next pre-flight checks it is back. "Warnings" = read it. "NEEDS ATTENTION" = check NinjaTrader that evening; if it says decisions did not match the recomputation or P&L is below the band, stop and talk before anything else.
- If the pre-flight message (about 09:17) says the strategy is not running: open NinjaTrader, reconnect, enable the strategy before 09:35. On full exchange holidays (New Year's Day, Good Friday, Christmas) it says the market is closed instead.
- Contract roll: in expiry week (NinjaTrader moves to the next contract on the Monday; MNQ stops trading at 09:30 ET on the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), on an evening Monday–Thursday **after 16:15 ET**, disable the strategy on the old contract, then enable it on the next one (e.g. MNQ 03-27) — never both at once on one account. Confirm the next morning's pre-flight shows the new instrument and `history_sessions` 14. The review warns from the week before, and needs attention on the Thursday evening if it has not been done. Next: 2026-12-14 to 12-17 (expiry 2026-12-18).
- Partial sessions: handled automatically (template + backup list). The review warns the day before and checks afterwards that no decision was made too late.
- After any reboot or NinjaTrader restart: check the strategy is enabled.

**Without Claude**: the strategy runs regardless. For the checks, run `python daily_review.py "C:\Users\linde\OneDrive\Documents\NinjaTrader 8\agent_exports"` (needs `pip install pandas numpy`), or just look at the heartbeat file's timestamp.

## 7. What to expect (1 contract, after costs)

From 2019-05 to 2026-10-14 on NinjaTrader's own data (the post-compile parity run of 2026-10-19): **1,666 trades, net $24,170** with 1 tick slippage per side and $1 commission, every full year positive (2020 $2,640 · 2021 $2,128 · 2022 $5,141 · 2023 $3,571 · 2024 $4,726 · 2025 $2,718 · 2026 to Oct 14 $3,214), t-stat 3.2, annualised Sharpe 1.16 (95% range 0.5–1.8; after a haircut for the choices made along the way, plan on about 0.8), worst drawdown **$3,912** (peak 2025-04-24, trough 2026-01-30 after 199 sessions, new high 2026-08-04 — 467 days under water). *(Figures refreshed 2026-10-20 from the current build; the earlier ones came from the pre-fix build.)*

- Typical year in the dollars of 2019–2026 (MNQ averaged 14,000 then, half today's level; 12-month month-block bootstrap of 2019-06 → 2026-09): median about **$3,000**; middle half of years $1,550–4,700; about 1 year in 12 loses; a great year (1 in 10) $6,400+. At today's price level the same returns are worth roughly twice that per micro — see the 16-year view below, which is the figure to plan with.
- About 0.9 trades a day, 41% winners, average win ≈ $160 vs average loss ≈ $90. Median trade is a small loss.
- Profit comes from a few days: the best 20 days (1% of sessions) carried 76% of the net; the 577 trades held to the 16:00 close made all the money, the 1,089 earlier exits lost in aggregate. Every signal must be taken; skipping days or taking profit early removes the edge. The strategy makes money in proportion to how much the market moves: break-even is roughly a 0.6% average daily open-to-close move; calm years (2019: 0.37%) make nothing.
- About 1 month in 3 is negative. Typical in-year drawdown $1,450, 1-in-20 year $3,000, observed worst $3,900. Expect ~3 months below the prior high in a normal year, up to 9.
- Robustness: still +$12,500 at 8 ticks slippage per side; each extra tick per side costs ≈ $220 a year (the earlier +$10,770 / $190 were computed with the commission taken twice — `robustness.py`, fixed 2026-10-20). Positive in every VIX regime and in both up- and down-trending index regimes (stronger in stressed markets) — from the 2026-10-15 research, no results file kept.

**The 16-year view (added 2026-10-16, `research\LONGHIST_RESULTS.txt`)**. The same rules on NQ 2010–2019 joined to MNQ 2019–2026, measured as a return on the contract's notional (so the 20× rise in price level cancels) and quoted per micro at the 2026 price level (notional ≈ $55,750):
- Expectation: about **8% of notional a year ≈ $4,700 per micro** at today's price and today's costs (the 7 years above alone: 11% ≈ $6,200). Median year $4,550; **1 year in 7 loses**; a bad year −$2,400 (5th percentile); a great year +$12,000. Daily t-stat 4.4 over 16 years.
- Drawdown: **worst 14% of notional ≈ $7,900 per micro**, July 2010 → May 2011, 195 sessions down and two years to a new high — twice the 2019–2026 worst. A within-year drawdown of $5,900 is a 1-in-20 year, $7,500 a 1-in-100 year. 2010 was a whipsaw market (big ranges, small net moves), the one regime that hurts this strategy and that nothing in the rules detects.
- Under the costs of the time (one tick was 1.7 bp of price in 2010, 0.18 bp now) 2010–2017 netted about zero and the July 2010 equity high was not exceeded until 2018. Cost drift is therefore the thing to watch: a doubling of the cost per trade moves the strategy a long way back toward that history.
- The money arrives in bursts: the best 1% of days are 77% of 16 years of profit; 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2025 are the bursts; 2013–2017 and 2019 are the flat years. Judge the strategy on 12 months, never on a quiet quarter.

**Sizing**: everything scales linearly, and the cushion is best stated as a share of notional (price × $2) so it rises with price. Per micro: **survival minimum 21% of notional ≈ $12,000** at today's price (1.5× the 16-year worst run; was $6,000 on 7 years of data); **prudent 38% of notional ≈ $21,000** (a repeat of the worst run uses 35–40% of it; was $10,000–12,000). $2,000/month ≈ 5 micros at today's expectation ≈ $106,000 prudent cushion; $5,000/month ≈ 13 micros ≈ $275,000. Switch from 10 micros to 1 full NQ contract only when you would otherwise hold 10 micros; at $0.50 per micro side vs $2.00 per NQ side and about 220 round trips a year it saves about $1,300 a year in commissions and changes nothing else.

**Scaling schedule** (2026-10-19, pre-registered in `research\SCALING_PLAN.txt`, results in `research\SCALING_RESULTS.txt`). For a self-funded account only, and only after the 60-clean-live-session gate. Contracts are reset at each month end from the account's equity, in whole micros. The verdict differs by starting equity, so it is a table:

| Starting equity | Rule (pre-registered verdict) | Micros at start | 5-year median equity (fixed 1 micro) | Chance of a 50% drawdown within 5 years | Typical / 1-in-20 drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $25,000 | survival: 1 micro per $11,700 (21% of notional) | 2 | $111,000 ($48,600) | 2.6% | 30% / 47% |

## 9. Decision log (what was tested and why the strategy is the way it is)

- **Overnight-range fade (first strategy)**: failed its pre-registered holdout (2019–2025 and MES). Retired. Kept in `research\onfade.py`.
- **Published strategies S1/S2/S3**: only S1 (noise-area momentum) passed the pre-registered bar. Adopted.
- **684-variant sweep** (6 families, picked on 2019–2022, validated once on 2023–2026): 430 of 432 momentum variants profitable out of sample — the family works, the parameter choice is noise (rank correlation 0.19). Published defaults kept. Consolidation plays (overnight fade, double tops at HOD/LOD, band reversion) all negative. ORB and VWAP-pullback failed validation.
- **Volatility-targeted sizing**: failed (no t-stat gain, doubled drawdown). 1 contract fixed.
- **Adaptive tuning experiment** (17 schemes: re-optimising on 6/12/24-month windows, anchored learning, equity-curve gating): none beat the fixed rules on both datasets; the tuner's picks ranked at the median of all candidates; fixed rules beat 97% of random re-selection. The one observation: the strategy does slightly better after a losing 20 sessions than after a winning one (weak, p 0.03/0.20) — the opposite of what tuning does. Nothing changed.
- **Independent re-implementation from the paper**: reproduced 1,489 of 1,491 trades, found the sigma specification gap, fixed (above). Parity re-proven: 1,666 of 1,666.
- **Overnight session**: failed discovery (−$14,220, t −3.6). Cash session only.
- **Fixed stop-loss and profit target (2026-10-16, pre-registered in `research\STOPS_PLAN.txt`, results in `research\STOPS_RESULTS.txt`)**: 83 variants (stop 0.25–2 and target 0.5–3 noise widths, with and without re-entry) on both datasets. A stop at 0.5–0.75 widths plus a target at 1.5 widths with re-entry looked excellent (validation t 2.25 → 3.06 on NinjaTrader bars, drawdown halved, better in 7 of 8 years) but **failed the pre-registered plateau test** — every neighbour falls below the baseline on one dataset — and on NQ 2010–2019 it is worse than the baseline once fills are realistic (t 1.76 vs 1.94) because it adds 50% more trades. Tight stops (0.25 widths, about $60–80) hurt everywhere; a target alone does nothing; a wide stop alone (0.75 widths, about $150–250 today) is neutral to mildly helpful on all three datasets but below the adoption bar. **Not adopted; nothing changed.** The loss limiter stays the 30-minute check: worst single trade in 1,666 was −$647, 6 trades ever lost more than $400.
- **Remaining structural ideas (2026-10-16, pre-registered in `research\ALPHA2_PLAN.txt`, results in `research\ALPHA2_RESULTS.txt`)**: FOMC-day handling (the strategy actually makes money on FOMC days; skipping them costs $1,345 out of sample), quad-witching skip (neutral), holding the 16:00 position to the next open (small positive drift, lower t-stat, gap and margin risk — not adopted), reversal replaced by flat (neutral), a daily loss stop at 1–3 noise widths (cuts out-of-sample net roughly in half — the best days often start with a loss), parameter ensembles (beat the baseline in sample, lose out of sample), and a narrower entry window (worse everywhere). **Seven ideas, nothing adopted.** This closes the list of rule changes with a plausible mechanism; what is left is sizing, costs, discipline and time.
- **Dealer gamma exposure (2026-10-16, pre-registered in `research\GEX_PLAN.txt`, results in `research\GEX_RESULTS.txt`)**: the one mechanism-backed idea from the literature (Baltussen et al.: intraday momentum is driven by dealers' hedging and is stronger when they are short gamma). Free daily SPX GEX history (Squeezemetrics, 2011–2026). The mechanism is visible in this strategy's own P&L — low-gamma days earn 2–5× more per day than high-gamma days in both periods on both datasets — which confirms the edge is the documented effect. But high-gamma days are still profitable, every filter gives up more profit than it saves, and gamma-based sizing adds variance in proportion (GEX correlates −0.4 with the day's range, so it is largely a volatility proxy). **Nothing adopted.** The series is kept for the annual re-validation as a diagnostic.
