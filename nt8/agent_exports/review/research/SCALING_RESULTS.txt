OBSERVATIONS (made after seeing the results; they do NOT change the verdict above, they are for the reader's judgement)
 a. R1 sits on the risk limit everywhere: about a 1-in-20 chance of losing half the account inside five years (2.6% / 4.6% / 5.7% by
    start; the differences mostly come from rounding down to whole micros at small sizes). An R1 account that starts at $50,000 grows
    past $105,000 in most paths, i.e. into the size at which R1 fails the bar. Treat R1 as the most a self-funded account should run,
    not as the target. Its typical drawdown is about a third of the account (median 30-33%, 1 in 20: 47-51%).
 b. R2 is comfortably inside the limits at every start (worst 0.04% chance of a 50% drawdown; typical drawdown 15-19%) and still
    multiplies fixed-1-micro's median five-year gain about 3x from $50,000 ($69,093 vs $23,611) and 7x from $105,000 ($167,714). From $25,000 it adds little (R2 holds 1 micro until
    equity reaches $42,370) and it can stall at zero micros after one bad stretch, as the historical path did in 2010-11.
 c. Fixed 1 micro is safest but its return on capital shrinks as the account grows (60-month median gain $23,600 whatever the start).
 d. Half-Kelly is far too aggressive for this payoff: the strategy's edge is a few big days, so leverage near Kelly turns the ordinary
    2010-11 or 2025-26 drawdowns into 50-60% account losses.
 e. All figures assume the 16-year edge persists at today's costs, a constant 2026 price level, no slippage growth with size, and no
    taxes or fees. The month-block bootstrap cannot produce a regime worse than 2010-11, the worst in the data.
