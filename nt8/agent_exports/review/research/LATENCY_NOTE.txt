ORDER LATENCY AND PC CLOCK - NOTE   (2026-10-19, read-only measurement; follows EXEC_PILOT_RESULTS.txt, which found that the real cost
of the strategy's market orders depends on the delay from the bar boundary to the order reaching the exchange)

BOTTOM LINE
 1. The PC clock is wrong and drifts, but it does NOT move the strategy's bars or decisions. NinjaTrader closes the live 5-minute bars
    on exchange (Rithmic) time; the clock error only skews PC-side timestamps (the NinjaTrader log, the heartbeat's updated_local).
 2. The strategy sends its order 48-68 ms after the true bar close (three decisions: 2026-10-16 10:00, 2026-10-19 11:30 and 12:30).
    The 16:00 flat took 598 ms (one observation). On a Rithmic account add about 20 ms to reach the exchange (median round trip
    39 ms on 42 of the user's BX49277-97 orders today), so about 70-90 ms from bar close to CME.
 3. At that latency the pilot's tape model puts the market-order cost at roughly +1.4 to +2.3 ticks/side on average (median +1),
    against the 1.0 the backtest assumes: about $100-290 a year per micro, mostly at the 10:00 decisions when data releases hit.
    The live gate's 1.5 ticks/side would be borderline on a real account. Sim101 cannot show this (its fills are internal).
 4. Nothing here needs a code change now. The clock should be fixed anyway (Sashkin's clicks, below) so that logs and timing
    measurements can be trusted.

CLOCK (measured)
 - SNTP against time.windows.com, pool.ntp.org, time.google.com and time.nist.gov (5 queries each, scratch script, offset = server
   minus PC): -714 to -721 ms at about 16:13 ET, all four within 7 ms -> the PC clock was 0.72 s FAST. Repeated at 16:27: -720/-725 ms.
   w32tm /stripchart at 16:05: -0.697 s (same sign convention).
 - Windows Time service (w32time): startup type Manual, status Stopped; configured source time.windows.com. It is triggered about once
   a day and steps the clock (System log, Kernel-General event 1, reason 1): 2026-10-16 06:25 (0.5 ms), 2026-10-17 12:58 (1 ms),
   2026-10-18 13:19 (+1.18 s: the clock had been SLOW after resuming from sleep), 2026-10-19 06:26 (-1.31 s: FAST).
 - Drift: from the 06:26 sync to 16:27 the clock gained 0.72 s = 71.9 ms per hour (about 1.7 s a day). Linear drift from 06:26
   predicts the three later measurements to within 15 ms (0.695 vs 0.697 at 16:05, about 0.70 vs 0.718 at ~16:13, 0.721 vs 0.720 at 16:27).
   Between syncs the clock therefore runs from about 0 to 1.3 s fast, plus steps of up to ~1 s after a resume from sleep (the RTC
   sets time to 0.5 s precision).
 - Side finding: the PC went to sleep twice (Fri 2026-10-16 23:53 -> Sat 08:14; Sat 2026-10-17 21:29 -> Sun 07:28; event 1 reason 2).
   Both were weekend nights with no session, but Section 5 says sleep off: on a weekday night it would disconnect NinjaTrader and
   break the heartbeat gate.

WHAT CLOCK NINJATRADER USES FOR LIVE BARS (inferred, two independent lines of evidence)
 - Order timing: NinjaTrader logs the strategy's "submitting order" at PC 10:00:00.305 (10-16), 11:30:00.432 and 12:30:00.490 (10-19).
   Corrected by the drift since that morning's sync (0.257 / 0.364 / 0.435 s) these are 48, 68 and 55 ms AFTER the true boundary. If
   bars were cut on the PC clock instead, the same orders would have to be 305-490 ms after the PC boundary with that gap growing in
   step with the clock offset (432 -> 490 ms as the offset grew 364 -> 435 ms); the exchange-time reading gives a near-constant
   48-68 ms, which is what a fixed processing time looks like. (An uncorrected reading of the log, done first today, wrongly suggested
   orders BEFORE the close: it ignored the drift.)
 - Bars: daily_review.py records that on 2026-10-16 only 14 of 197 live-recorded bars differed from NinjaTrader's historical bars
   (one open by 11 ticks). A 0.26-0.69 s bar shift that day would have changed far more of them.
 - NinjaTrader's documentation does not say which applies to Rithmic: staff answers say the timestamp source depends on the connection
   (forum 101755, 1211795), and recommend syncing the Windows clock (forum 1092182, 1104211). The evidence above is the answer for
   this setup. Uncertainty on the latencies: about +/-20 ms (the drift rate is from one interval; 10-16's rate is assumed the same).

LATENCY CHAIN, true time after the exchange-time bar close
   strategy decides and submits         48 / 68 / 55 ms (16:00 flat: 598 ms, one observation - the session-end path does more work)
   Sim101 fill                          +about 120 ms after submit (the simulator's own delay; not a real cost)
   Rithmic account, to the exchange     +about 20 ms (half the 39 ms median Submitted->Accepted round trip; n = 42, 9-79 ms)
   => about 70-90 ms on a real account (about 620 ms for the 16:00 flat)

COST IMPLICATION (EXEC_PILOT_RESULTS.txt Part B, 124 sides of real 2026 MNQ trades; 1 tick/side = $226 a year per micro)
   latency      mean cost ticks/side    median    excess over the model's 1.0, per micro per year
   50 ms        +1.44                   +1.00     about $100
   100 ms       +2.29                   +1.00     about $290
   250 ms       +5.42                   +2.00     about $1,000   (the 16:00 flat sits beyond this; the pilot's 16:00 side cost +3.25 at 250 ms)
   Almost all of the excess comes from the 10:00 decisions (17 of 124 sides). 124 sides is a small sample: treat these as ranges.

OPTIONS, cheapest first
 a. Fix the clock (Sashkin's clicks; Claude does not change system settings). It does not change trading, it makes every timestamp
    honest and every future latency check simple:
      1. Settings > Time & language > Date & time: "Set time automatically" On, then "Sync now".
      2. To stop the daily 1-2 s swings: Windows key, type services.msc, Enter > Windows Time > Startup type "Automatic" > Start > OK.
         (With the service running Windows syncs on its own schedule; the drift is about 1.7 s a day without it.)
    Best done outside 09:30-16:00 ET. After it, the pre-flight's heartbeat age and the log times are exact.
 b. Power settings: Settings > System > Power (or Power & battery) > Screen and sleep > "When plugged in, put my device to sleep
    after": Never.
 c. NinjaTrader settings: nothing needed for the bars (they are on exchange time).
 d. Code (not now): the 16:00 flat's 598 ms is the one slow path. Submitting before the end-of-session file writes would cut about
    half a second on roughly 80 sides a year (the trades held to the close), worth perhaps $50-100 a year per micro by the pilot's
    numbers. Measure a few more 16:00 exits first; any change needs a pre-registered plan, an audit and a parity run before F5.
 e. The larger cost lever stays the pilot's passive-limit idea (limit 1 tick better, 60 s, then market), which needs more tick data
    and its own pre-registered test.
 f. The real cost will first be measurable on a Rithmic account's fills (read the 10:00 decisions separately). With the clock fixed,
    the log's "submitting order" minus the bar time gives the latency directly on every trade.
