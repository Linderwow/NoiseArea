   size is optimal until the Bulenox drawdown locks and size may grow only out of the cushion afterwards; Graham Capital and Kaminski-Lo
   show cutting size after drawdowns hurts trend strategies; time stops and breakeven stops hurt (Davey). Not tested: they are
   either already in the graveyard or sizing rules for after the 60-session gate.
 - Decay since publication (May 2024): the SPY version has stopped working in three independent re-tests (Paz SSRN 7290621: Sharpe
   0.39 out of sample; Brusco: 2025 -4.9%, 2026 -12.9%; ES likewise). On Nasdaq futures it has held: VigorTas NQ +$8,130 per micro

   the notional, they quote the dollars of each year; the 2019-2026 Analyzer drawdown ($4,171) matches theirs.

WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY
 - Long-only (V1): the 16-year drawdown falls from $7,906 to $2,900, but on markets nobody had looked at it is WORSE than both
   sides (MES, MYM, M2K), and on the second MNQ source its MAR is lower (1.36 vs 1.68). On a Bulenox 50K account it keeps the
   account open far more often (89% vs 66% after a year) but pays less over two years ($1,846 vs $3,038) and almost never
   qualifies inside the 30-day window (0.7% vs 3.7%). The shorts made the big money in 2018, 2020 and 2022 (+$12,226, +$6,159,
   +$3,169 per micro) and lost it in 2010 and 2016 (-$5,274, -$2,530) - Concretum's FAQ also calls the shorts the crisis leg.
 - Consistent but not significant (pass D2 on all three Nasdaq data sets): V6 lunch pause (MAR 2.05 vs 1.35 on MNQ, also better on
   the fresh markets, Bulenox +$465 over two years), V2 shorts at half size, V5 exit gate, V7 EMA confirmation. Their 16-year gains
   are within what reshuffling the months produces, so none can be adopted on this evidence; they are the candidates should a
   future pre-registered forward test be set up.
 - V3 (90-day lookback) was the authors' best idea on SPY; on Nasdaq it helps 2010-2019 and hurts 2019-2026 (MNQ MAR 0.73 vs 1.35;
   2025 +$153, 2026 -$432), and 60 / 120 days do the same - no plateau. V4 fast-alpha and V9 no-gap make things worse.
