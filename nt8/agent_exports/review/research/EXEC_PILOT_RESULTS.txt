BOTTOM LINE
1. The $20.86 purchase (one year of MNQ 1-second bars): NO-GO. One-second bars cannot measure this strategy's fill cost. Every
   decision fills in one of the busiest seconds of the day (xx:00:00 / xx:30:00, the 10:00:00 data releases, the 16:00:00 close).
   There, the adverse extreme of the first second sits a median 9 ticks (mean 18) from the backtest's price. On the same tape, the
   1-second estimate overstates the market-order cost by +12.9 ticks/side (calibration (i), bar 0.25) and the passive saving by
   +10 (calibration (ii)): a year of them would measure the bar granularity, not the fills. The question needs tick data. The PC already holds 3.5 months of it (MNQ trades with aggressor
   side, Jan 2 - Apr 16 2026, used here). More tick data would be a separate purchase - his call.
2. Sim101 execution trial: NO (by the plan's rule, and on the merits: NinjaTrader's simulator fills inside the platform, with no
   trip to the exchange, and fills a resting limit on touch, so it would show neither the latency cost nor a realistic passive
   fill; and it would change the execution while the 20-session gate is measuring it).
3. The cost of the current market orders depends on the order latency (time from the first trade after the bar close to the order
   reaching CME, through NinjaTrader and Rithmic). This setup's latency is unknown. Part B, 124 sides:
       latency      0 ms   25 ms   50 ms  100 ms  250 ms (plan)  1000 ms
       mean cost   +0.59   +1.21   +1.44   +2.29   +5.42          +4.38   ticks/side vs the backtest price (model: 1.0)
       median       0.00   +1.00   +1.00   +1.00   +2.00          +1.00
   Pre-registered verdict at 250 ms: BREACH (+5.42, 95% CI +1.28..+9.62, above the 1.5 gate). At 25-50 ms it would be BORDERLINE
   (+1.2..+1.4) and at <= 10 ms ADEQUATE (+0.3..+1.0). Almost all of the excess is in the 10:00 decisions (17 of 124 sides): 10:00
   costs +1.8 / +3.4 / +8.9 / +24.9 ticks at 25 / 50 / 100 / 250 ms. The other 107 sides (10:30-15:30 and the close) stay at
   +1.1 / +1.1 / +1.2 / +2.3, close to the model. At 10:00:00 several scheduled US data releases hit. The backtest fills at the
   first trade of that second, before the reaction; a real order arrives after it. Seven 10:00 entries moved >= 40 ticks within
   250 ms (six against the order, one +152 in its favour). 17 sides cannot tell whether that is a systematic bias or just noise.
   In dollars (453 sides a year from the reference list, so 1 tick/side = $226/yr per micro): the excess over the model is about
   $100/yr per micro at 50 ms, $290 at 100 ms and $1,000 at 250 ms, against a 16-year expectation of about $4,700.
4. Passive alternative (limit at the decision price or 1 tick better, held 10-60 s, then market): all six pre-registered variants
   clear the PROMISING bar on the tape with conservative (trade-through) fills. The best is a limit 1 tick better than the decision
   price, held 60 s: +6.27 ticks/side at 250 ms (CI +3.32..+9.42), 96% filled. The 5 misses cost -33 ticks each on average. The
   runners' entries still save +4.9. Post hoc, at 25-50 ms it saves +1.7 overall, and +2.2..+2.4 without the 10:00 decisions
   (CI +1.2..+3.3). At 0 ms it saves nothing (-0.3). It is the best of six, so it is biased upward. 124 sides with 4-5 misses
   cannot price its tail: the worst single 60-second miss on a runaway move cost 72 to 159 ticks, depending on the latency
   assumed. Under the plan, PROMISING buys a 1-second purchase only if 1-second bars can measure it. They cannot, so this stays
   an untested idea that needs more tick data and a one-variant pre-registered test.

CHECKS (plan section 6)
 (1) R equals Databento's own 1-minute bar on 133 of 142 sides. The 9 others differ by 1-5 ticks on the boundary trade (not
     explained by receive vs exchange order). Taking the 1-minute value instead moves the mean market cost by +0.07 tick/side and
     the savings by nothing (they do not depend on R).
 (2) Tape minus reference-file price: one constant back-adjust offset per contract (Dec-25 -1063.00; H6 -809.75; M6 -595.50); 100%
     exact on Part A's front-contract sides. In Part B, 87% of the 10:30-15:30 sides match exactly, 53% at 10:00 and 0 of 20 at the
     16:00 close (mean 5.3 ticks apart). NinjaTrader's 16:00 bar close is on average 3.5 ticks better for the strategy than the
     tape's last trade before 16:00 (13 of 20 sides), so measured against NinjaTrader's own prices the market cost is +1.29 at 0 ms
     (not +0.59) and +6.12 at 250 ms. Boundary check (exec_pilot_ntbars_output.txt, 118 sides to Apr 9): NinjaTrader's price
     matches the tape's boundary trade best about 10 ms before the exchange-time boundary (mid-day 92%, 10:00 76%, close 28%; at
     the boundary itself 90% / 71% / 17%; a second later 10% / 6% / 0%). Its historical bars appear to be cut a few milliseconds
     early, which receive-time stamping would do. In the 16:00 closing burst even that moves the price; in all 118 cases
     NinjaTrader's price did trade within 1 s of the boundary.
 (3) Worked examples with raw trades printed (exec_pilot_output.txt, CHECKS): 2026-01-02 13:00 entry, 2026-01-06 16:00 flat, and
     the Part A Dec-5 10:00 entry. Hand-checked: 01-06 flat, sell, R = 25,813.25, touch at +250 ms bid 25,811.00 = +9 ticks.

LIVE Sim101 COMPARISON (4 sides, NinjaTrader-simulated fills)
 2026-10-16: +1.50 ticks/side over 2 sides (same-day review against live-recorded bars). 2026-10-19 (recomputed): long 11:30
 29,951.00 vs next-bar open 29,950.00 = +4 ticks; exit 12:30 29,857.25 vs 29,856.00 = -5 ticks. Mean +0.50 tick/side. That is in
 line with the tape's 0-10 ms rows, as expected for a simulator with no exchange round trip, but 4 sides say nothing statistically.
 Consequence for the gate (an observation, nothing changed): the Sim101 fill-cost gate (<= 1.5 ticks/side) will probably pass and
 still not measure the real-money cost, because that cost depends on the trip to the exchange. The first real measure will be the
 fills on a Rithmic account, with the 10:00 decisions read separately.
