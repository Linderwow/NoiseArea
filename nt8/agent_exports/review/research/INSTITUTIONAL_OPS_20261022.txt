2. AUTOMATED START AND RECOVERY (question 1)
========================================================================================================================================
WHAT INSTITUTIONS DO
 - Treat the trading process as a service with a defined recovery objective: supervised start (process manager / scheduler),
   automatic reconnect, state recovery from a position/order source of truth, and an operator runbook. RTS 6 Art. 16 requires that
   monitoring staff be reachable at all times, with communication channels tested periodically [S12] (RTS 6 also has a
   business-continuity article, Art. 14, not re-read for this note).
 - Recovery restores the intended position from an authoritative record and reconciles against the broker/exchange before
   trading resumes (RTS 6 Art. 17(3): reconcile own logs with venue/broker records, in real time where they report in real
   time [S13]). Exchanges provide Cancel-on-Disconnect so resting orders do not outlive a dead session [S1 sec 1.4, S16].
 - No production deployments or restarts during trading hours (change freeze); Knight Capital's 2012 loss ($460 million in about
   45 minutes) began with an unreviewed deployment and was worsened by an incident response with no written procedure [S9].

WHAT NINJATRADER 8 CAN AND CANNOT DO (facts)
 - Connections: the connection's "Connect on start up" option makes NinjaTrader connect automatically when it starts [S28]. With
   Multi-provider on (as here), staff say auto-connect is per connection via that option [S29].
 - Strategies do NOT come back enabled. Workspaces restore strategies in a disabled state; NinjaTrader support has said there is no
   supported way to enable a strategy automatically, that strategies must be consciously enabled by the user, and that strategies
   cannot be autostarted after the application has been closed; auto-recovery only covers a momentary connection loss while the
   program keeps running (feature request SFT-392, not implemented) [S30, S31, S32]. A 2025-26 Discourse thread shows the same
   behaviour with no staff fix [S33].
 - NinjaTrader 8.1 requires the NinjaTrader account login at every launch; staff (2023) said there is no supported workaround;
   a "remember me" request is tracked as SFT-5780 [S34, S35]. Not re-checked for 8.1.8.4 - see "could not confirm".
 - Connection loss inside a running program (Tools > Options > Strategies > NinjaScript > On connection loss) [S20-S24]:
     Keep Running: keeps running, logs the disconnect.
     Recalculate (current): if the data feed is down longer than "Disconnect delay seconds", or the order feed is down and the
       strategy tries to place an order, the strategy stops; when the connection returns it restarts and recalculates its position.
     Stop Strategy: stops after the delay, does nothing on reconnect.
     Restarts are limited by "Number of restart attempts" (default 4) within "Restarts within x minutes" (default 5): beyond that the
       strategy stops for good [S22, S23]. Staff have suggested 10-20 attempts within ~10 minutes for flaky connections, and warned
       that very high values effectively switch the handling off [S24, S25].
 - Start behaviour (current: Immediately submit, synchronize account): on enable, the strategy computes its position from the
   historical replay and a market order brings the account into line [S26]. That is exactly the "recover from source of truth"
   step, and also means a late enable joins the trade late at market (the "switched on late" case of this week).

3. REAL-TIME MONITORING, ALERTING, KILL SWITCH, PRE-TRADE LIMITS (question 2)

WHAT INSTITUTIONS DO
 - Real-time monitoring of every algorithm while orders are being sent, by the trader AND an independent risk function; automated
   alerts within five seconds of the event; staff reachable at all times, contact channels tested periodically (RTS 6 Art. 16)
   [S12]. Post-trade controls run continuously and reconcile own records with the broker/venue (Art. 17) [S13].
 - Pre-trade controls on every order: price collars, maximum order value and volume, maximum message rates, repeated-execution
   throttles that disable the system until a named person re-enables it, automatic blocking of orders that breach risk limits
   (RTS 6 Art. 15 [S11]; FIA 2024 sections 1.1 max order size - "Systems should prevent orders from being placed in cases where no
   order size limits have been set", 1.2 max intraday position, 1.3 price tolerance, 3.1 market-data reasonability (time since last
   update, deviation -> alert and block), 3.2 repeated automated execution limits [S1]); SEC 15c3-5 requires pre-set credit/capital
   thresholds, rejection of orders exceeding price or size parameters, duplicate-order checks, and an annual documented review [S7].
 - Kill switch: one action that blocks new orders and cancels working ones, ideally separate from the trading application and
   usable by the person responsible for risk (FIA 1.5 [S1]; RTS 6 Art. 12 [S10]; CME Globex Kill Switch and Cancel on Disconnect
   [S15, S16]). Drop-copy reconciliation (an independent copy of executions compared with the firm's own records) as the early
   warning (FIA 4.1 [S1]).
 - Lesson from the SEC Knight order [S9]: the firm's monitor (PMON) "relied entirely on human monitoring and did not generate
   automated alerts", did not display limits, and 97 automated error e-mails sent before the open were not designed as alerts and
   were not read. Alerts must be few, specific, and wired to someone who will act.

4. EXECUTION: TCA, LATENCY, CLOCK, ORDER TYPE (question 3)

4.1 TRANSACTION COST ANALYSIS
 What institutions do: measure implementation shortfall (Perold 1988): paper portfolio at the decision price vs the real one; the
 benchmark is the decision/arrival price (mid when the order is released), split into delay cost (decision -> arrival), execution
 cost (arrival -> fill) and opportunity cost (unfilled), reported by time of day, venue, order type and market condition, and fed
 back into pre-trade cost estimates and sizing [S52, S53]. VWAP is the softer benchmark; arrival price is the honest one for an
 alpha strategy.
 Free steps: in the strategy's trade log add, per order: decision bar close (exchange time) and price; best bid/ask at submit
 (GetCurrentBid/GetCurrentAsk); a System.Diagnostics.Stopwatch reading at decision, submit, Accepted and Filled (OnOrderUpdate /
 OnExecutionUpdate) - Stopwatch intervals are immune to PC clock offset; the fill price. Then
   delay cost  = side x (arrival mid - decision price)      execution cost = side x (fill - arrival mid)
   latency     = Stopwatch(submit -> filled)                 reported separately for 10:00 vs other decisions, release days vs not.
 Expected benefit: the first Bulenox fills will show whether the +2.6-tick 10:00 cost is latency (delay cost) or the release-second
 spread (execution cost); that decides whether latency spending (section 7) could ever pay. Low effort; needs the usual
 pre-registration / audit / parity run because it touches the strategy file.

4.2 LATENCY
 What institutions do: co-locate in CME's Aurora data centre (sub-millisecond to the matching engine), measure tick-to-trade p50/p99
 continuously, keep logging and disk I/O off the order path, freeze changes in trading hours.
 This setup: the chain is ~48-68 ms bar close -> order submitted (includes market data arriving at the PC) + ~20 ms to CME
 (LATENCY_NOTE). Rithmic's datacenters include Aurora (CME co-location) [S19]; NinjaTrader staff list Rithmic live servers in
 Chicago, Europe, Singapore and US East [S27]; prop-firm guides put Bulenox on "Rithmic Paper Trading" / Chicago [S47]. Chicago is
 the right choice for a US user; whether the Bulenox system offers any other gateway, and whether one would be faster from this
 house, was not confirmed.
 Free steps: (a) keep the wired Ethernet (already); (b) nothing CPU- or disk-heavy at :00/:30 during RTH - the hourly autopush at
 :07 with an offline NinjaScript build is outside the decision minutes, keep it so (or move it to after 16:05); (c) LATENCY_NOTE
 item d: submit the 16:00 flat before the end-of-session file writes (598 ms observed) - pre-registered change; (d) keep the
 NinjaTrader instance lean (charts on tick data and Tick Replay cost CPU; the strategy needs neither).
 Expected benefit: by LIMIT_RESULTS, 75 -> 25 ms saves ~0.57 tick on ~67 10:00 entries = ~$19 a year per micro; the free steps above
 mainly protect against the expensive tail (+6.2 ticks at 150 ms).

4.3 CLOCK SYNCHRONISATION
 What institutions do: regulated firms keep business clocks within a stated tolerance of UTC and document it. FINRA Rule 6820: 50
 ms for most business clocks, synchronised before the open and checked during the day, with a log [S6]. MiFID II RTS 25: 1 ms for
 algorithmic/other trading, 100 microseconds for HFT [S14]. Large desks use PTP or GPS appliances; Microsoft supports 1 ms on
 Windows 10/Server 2016+ only with < 0.1 ms network latency to the source, 50 ms with < 5 ms, and 1 s with < 100 ms one-way [S3].
 This setup: decisions are unaffected - NinjaTrader's "Data by provider" table lists Rithmic real-time timestamps as Native
 (stamped by the source, not the PC) [S17], consistent with LATENCY_NOTE's measurement. The PC clock still drives the log,
 heartbeat ages, any DateTime.Now logic and latency arithmetic. Today it is ~42 ms fast with 4,096-s polling, and this PC has
 UpdateInterval 360,000 (the stand-alone default [S4]; Microsoft's high-accuracy value is 100) and FrequencyCorrectRate 4 (high-
 accuracy value 2): slow phase correction, which fits a clock still 40+ ms off five minutes after a "successful" sync.
 Free steps (Microsoft "Configuring systems for high accuracy" [S5]; admin Command Prompt, outside RTH, NinjaTrader flat):
   reg add HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\W32Time\Config /v MinPollInterval /t REG_DWORD /d 6 /f
   reg add HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\W32Time\Config /v MaxPollInterval /t REG_DWORD /d 6 /f
   reg add HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\W32Time\Config /v UpdateInterval /t REG_DWORD /d 100 /f
   reg add HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\W32Time\Config /v FrequencyCorrectRate /t REG_DWORD /d 2 /f
   reg add HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\W32Time\TimeProviders\NtpClient /v SpecialPollInterval /t REG_DWORD /d 64 /f
   w32tm /config /manualpeerlist:"time.google.com,0x9 time.windows.com,0xb" /syncfromflags:manual /update
   net stop w32time && net start w32time
   w32tm /resync /rediscover
 (0x1 = use SpecialPollInterval, 0x8 = client mode, 0x2 = use as fallback only; with two servers Microsoft recommends marking one
 as fallback [S4]. time.google.com had the shortest round trip here, 26 ms. Google smears leap seconds; mixing smeared and
 unsmeared servers only matters at a leap second, none is scheduled - not re-verified today.) Keep the service Automatic.
 Verify: "w32tm /query /status" shows Poll Interval 6 (64s); "w32tm /stripchart /computer:time.nist.gov /samples:10 /dataonly"
 and the same against time.google.com should agree within a few ms of zero. Expected: |offset| of a few ms, bounded by half the
 26-ms round trip (~13 ms) in the worst asymmetric case; drift between 64-s polls at 72 ms/h is ~1.3 ms. Optional: silence the
 VMICTimeProvider warnings with reg add ...\TimeProviders\VMICTimeProvider /v Enabled /t REG_DWORD /d 0 /f.
 Expected benefit: honest timestamps everywhere (no drift corrections in latency studies, the daily review's 2-s tolerance could
 be tightened), and a prerequisite for any PC-timer logic. No trading impact.

4.4 RESTING / LIMIT EXECUTION FOR MOMENTUM ENTRIES
 What institutions do: passive/limit execution for patient flow (rebalancing, mean reversion, liquidity provision); aggressive or
 marketable-limit execution for urgent, momentum-type alpha, because limit orders carry adverse selection (filled when the market
 comes back) and non-execution risk (missed when it runs) - Handa & Schwartz 1996 [S54]. CME Globex already wraps market orders as
 "Market Order with Protection": fills only up to the best opposite price +/- protection points, any rest stays as a limit at the
 protection price [S18b] - a built-in price collar.
 This setup: already tested - a 1-tick-better limit held 60 s loses 5.7 ticks a side; delaying the market order 5/15/30 s loses
 7.5/3.4/1.7 (LIMIT_RESULTS). Consistent with the literature: keep market orders.
 Clock-time crowding: activity rises ~20 % at 5- and 10-minute marks in equities (Broussard & Nikiforov 2014 [S55]); algorithmic
 trades cluster in the first 100 ms of each second, raising volatility but not significantly changing liquidity (Muravyev & Picard
 2022 [S56]). At 10:00 ET the bigger effect is the scheduled data release (ISM manufacturing/services at 10:00 a.m. ET [S57];
 JOLTS and others also at 10:00).
 Untested idea (free with the trade windows already bought, D:\databento_archive\mnq_trades_1000_windows_2019-2026): make the 10:00
 decision on the last exchange-time tick after ~09:59:59.7 and send the order then, i.e. before the release second. Pre-register:
 decision-flip rate vs the true bar close (expected tiny), cost vs the model's next-bar open, and a kill rule. Not found as an
 institutional practice in a primary source (retail "news filters" pause trading around releases instead; no broker-algo
 documentation found).

WHAT INSTITUTIONS DO
 - One order per decision, allocated to accounts afterwards (block order + average-price allocation), or one parent per account
   from a single order manager; positions of every account reconciled continuously against the broker's records (drop copy) with
   alerts and an automatic stop on mismatch (FIA 4.1 [S1]; RTS 6 Art. 17 [S13]).
 - Daily loss limits per account monitored with alerts before the limit, plus an automatic block at the limit (FIA 4.2 [S1]; RTS 6
   Art. 15(4)-(5) [S11]); limits set by someone independent of the trader where possible.

THIS SETUP
 - Bulenox eval: own strategy instance (Contracts 6, daily loss stop $1,000 per the plan), not a Replikanto follower - the
   strategy's desync guard and loss stop apply to it directly. Bulenox's own server-side rules apply on top (not restated here:
   account parameters come from the user and Bulenox, not from research).
 - Rithmic risk parameters: Rithmic support's instructions (via AMP) describe a trader-set daily loss limit in the Rithmic Trader
   Dashboard (Auto Liquidate, Loss Limit, "Liquidate Only" which blocks new trades until manually reset) [S51]; another broker says
   Rithmic no longer lets users set their own parameters [S51b]. Prop firms may lock them. Free to check in R|Trader Pro with the
   Bulenox login; do not rely on it unless it is visibly active.

FREE STEPS
 A. When Masters are copied with Replikanto (FlowBots documentation [S46, S47b]): Follower Guard on - "Flatten the follower if it
    becomes out of sync after X seconds" (default 10), "...if it is in the opposite direction after X seconds" (default 5), "Cancel
    the follower entry order if it is not filled" after X seconds (default 5), flatten on order errors (rejected, could not be
    submitted, change/cancel errors; default 3 s), "Disarm followers" (default on), e-mail to the user (needs NinjaTrader's default
    e-mail share service), "Refresh a Rithmic account's position before acting on it" (default on), "Lock follower settings while in
    a trade" (default on). Limitations to know: remote (network/remote-node) followers are not marked out of sync; actions apply to
    the tab's instrument only; Follower Guard has no daily loss limit (product page lists none).
 B. Extend the watchdog (section 3 A) to every account's heartbeat: account_position vs desired per account, all accounts flat
    after 16:02, escalation at 16:30/16:45 ET - the drop-copy analogue that does not depend on the copier being right.
 C. Measure copier slippage before scaling: for each copied decision compare leader fill and follower fill (same decision, same
    second). A follower order leaves only after the leader's fill is reported; at 10:00, where cost rises from +2.6 to +6.2 ticks
    between 75 and 150 ms, a few extra tens of ms matter. If followers pay materially more, run one NoiseAreaStrategy instance per
    account instead (each with its own desync guard and loss stop) - more instances to watch, but no copy delay and no leader
    dependency. (Whether Bulenox treats this differently from copying was not checked; their 2026-10-15 e-mail approved automation
    on all account stages per NoiseArea_Strategy.md.)
 D. Account-level loss guard independent of the strategy (catches stray manual orders, which the strategy cannot see): NinjaScript
    Account.Get(AccountItem.RealizedProfitLoss / UnrealizedProfitLoss) or OnAccountItemUpdate in a small AddOn, or simply include
    account P&L in the heartbeat for the watchdog to alert on [S38b, S38c]. Alerting-only first; flattening logic only after its
    own test.

EXPECTED BENEFIT
 - A-B: a diverged follower is flattened and reported in seconds instead of discovered at the review; this is the main way copied
   prop accounts are lost. C: avoids scaling a hidden per-follower cost. D: covers the stray-order case at account level.
