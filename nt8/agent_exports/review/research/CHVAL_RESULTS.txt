WHERE THE "IMPROVEMENT" CAME FROM (whole period, $ per micro, MNQ 29,150)
  Net effect: +$2,141 in 16 years (+$132/yr) - the sum of +$41,003 of giveback saved on 435 sessions and -$38,861 of upside lost
  (or extra re-entry cost) on 467 sessions: nearly a wash, with 866 re-entries after a CH exit.
  Big-move sessions (> 1 daily sigma, where ALL of NoiseArea's money is made): -$6,957 (it cuts winners); other sessions +$9,098.
  Long trades +$5,373, short trades -$1,711. By hour of the first CH exit: 13:00 +$1,240 and 14:00 +$2,643; 12:00 -$624, 15:00 -$1,336.
  The lower maximum drawdown ($6,963 vs $8,268) comes from two episodes - 2010-11 ($8,268 -> $6,963) and 2025-26 ($4,800 -> $4,172) -
  while CH creates two drawdowns the live rules do not have that deep (2014-15 $3,665, 2011-12 $3,456). Worst day unchanged (-$2,251 vs
  -$2,248); 5% CVaR -$519 vs -$530.
  By window: NQ 2010-19 MAR 0.535 vs 0.443; MNQ 2019-22 2.170 vs 2.229 (worse); MNQ 2023-26 1.614 vs 1.282 (better) - the gain is
  concentrated in particular periods, not steady.
  Bulenox (same bootstrap): value per slot $3,317 vs $3,001, 5-account fleet $15,120 vs $13,700 at the full edge; $639 vs $552 at half
  - positive, but built on the same non-robust difference.

CONCLUSION: the exit's signal is real (it beats random exits) but its trade-off - saving giveback on reversal days versus cutting the
big trend days that pay for everything - does not come out ahead consistently across periods, years or instruments. Under the
protocol that is a dead idea, not a forward-test candidate.
