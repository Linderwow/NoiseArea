PART B - AUTOMATED RULE SEARCH
  Rounds 1-2: 2,688 single-condition rules + 6,702 two-condition rules = 9,390 rules on NQ 2010-2019 (2,201 days; decision times
  09:45-15:30, four exits, long/short, 49 conditions: return since the open, last-30-minute return, VWAP side and distance,
  opening-range position (15/30/60 min), prior-day high/low/close, gap, first-30-minute move, prior-day and 5-day returns, trend
  efficiency, volatility ratio, overnight range, Monday/Friday, FOMC day).
  With 9,390 tries, the best Sharpe expected from luck alone is 1.35 a year; the best rule found scored exactly that (long at 15:30 to
  the close on Fridays after a top-third week; deflated Sharpe 0.500 = luck level, the bar is 0.95). No survivors, so rounds 3-4 had
  nothing to validate or refine; the 2023-2026 holdout is untouched.
  Audits: (1) deflated-Sharpe bar under pure noise - the best of 1,000 random rules cleared it in 0 of 400 batches (the plan expected
  about 5%): the bar uses one rule's standard error while the best-of-N varies less, so it is stricter than nominal - it lets no luck
  through, and a real rule only slightly better than luck could be missed; the best real rule sat exactly at luck level, nowhere near
  the bar either way. (2) Rule engine vs a plain loop on 100 random rule-days: 0 differences. (3) Part A re-computed by separate plain
  loops (rulemine_check.py): all five strategies match day for day.

WHAT THIS MEANS
  - Searching more is not the missing ingredient: 9,390 generated rules plus every source strategy reachable here produce nothing beyond
    what luck produces, except the two edges already found - NoiseArea itself and the FOMC-eve overnight long.
  - The untested directions that could still add something need data we do not have: order flow at the bands, and cheaper execution
    (limit-then-market entries) - both need MNQ tick data (a purchase, Sashkin's call).
